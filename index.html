The shape of the week

Six threads woven through eight days.

We're keeping the exact activities a little mysterious — last year's participants told us the surprises were the best part. But here's the shape of what you're walking into. Two rhythms run through the whole week: one side leans into physical challenge, outdoor tasks and teamwork. The other side leans into silence, breath and body awareness. Together they make space for you to actually meet yourself.

🌿

Digital detox

No phone, almost the whole week. The non-negotiable heart of this exchange. On Day 1 we do a small ritual together — phones go in a box, and they come back to you on the last workshop day. More on this below — read it carefully.

🧘

Meditation & breath

Quiet practices woven through each day. Morning body-and-mind sessions. Breathwork. A different kind of stillness than you're used to. You'll leave with practices you can actually keep using.

🔥

Challenge

Moments designed to take you just outside your comfort zone — in a group that has your back. Trust your body. Surprise yourself.

🌲

Outdoors

Forest. Trails. Long walks. Real rural France, met on foot. Most of the week happens under the sky.

🤫

Silence

One of the days gets quieter than the others. More than that, you'll find out when you're there. People who've done this before say it's the part that lingers most.

🤝

Bonding

Shared meals, long evenings, mixed international teams. Bonfires, games, songs — all the analog stuff that's easier to remember than a feed. Six countries, one long table.

The big one · Read this carefully

This is a no-phone week. For real.

When you apply, you're signing up for almost a full week without your phone. We mean it. We don't take phones away on arrival — you'll have them through travel and the first morning, when you're settling in and finding your feet. Then, halfway through Day 1, we do a small ritual together. Phones into a box. Box closed. You get them back on the last workshop day, before you head home. This isn't a "try to use it less" situation — it's the whole point of the project.

Programme

Eight days, one rhythm.

Every day follows the same shape: a soft morning, deeper work before lunch, a bigger workshop or expedition in the afternoon, reflection + shared meals in the evening. You always know what's next. Tap a day below.

Go deeper · Four moments

The activities people talk about months later.

Day 3 · Voluntary

Cold exposure

The hot tub on site becomes an ice bath. Up to 5 of you in at once, a partner standing outside for each person. 60 seconds max. Wim Hof–style breathing to prepare. You can watch, you can try, you can stop. No one is pushing you past your edge — not even you.

Day 6 · Silence day

A whole day without words.

No talking from after breakfast until dinner. Meals eaten slowly, alone or beside someone. A guided walking meditation. A Vipassana-style sit. Time moves strangely. Most people underestimate this day. Then it becomes the part they mention first, later.

Day 4 · All day

Bujaleuf → Peyrat-le-Château.

A mindful long walk through the Haute-Vienne countryside. Coffee at the village bakery. Lunch in local homes. Film at a tiny village cinema (think Seven Years in Tibet). Dinner with Familles Rurales. Bus home, probably half-asleep and full-hearted.

Day 5 · Evening

Deepdance.

Lights low. Eyes closed. You're guided through flow, joy, chaos, sorrow, peace — through movement alone. No one watching. Led by a member of the Spanish team with years of experience holding this kind of space. It's a lot. In the best way.

One day to know about · Silence Day

A whole day without words.

Somewhere in the middle of the week, one of the days gets quiet. Really quiet. No talking from after breakfast until we come back together in the evening. You'll eat in silence, walk in silence, be alone with your own head for longer than you're probably used to. It sounds intense. It usually is — and also usually ends up being the part people mention first, months later.

Things that help: a notebook, a warm layer, and being honest if it gets too much. Quiet spaces are set aside for stepping out. Joyce, who looks after wellbeing on site, is around the whole day if you need a chat. Nothing about the day is about performance — you're not meditating correctly or incorrectly. You're just not talking for a while.

It's a gift. Let it be one.

Where you'll live

On a hill, in the middle of nothing important.

Bujaleuf, Haute-Vienne — rural south-central France. You'll stay in furnished safari tents on a hillside, with a big communal space for workshops, shared meals and evenings. Forest trails at the door. A lake with a sandy beach a short walk away. A tiny village with a bakery and supermarket.

Your home for the week

You'll share a simple tent with 2 or 3 others from across the group. Tents are same-gender. Real beds, soft light, hot showers a short walk away. Yes to table tennis, yes to swimming in the lake if September is kind.

Still thinking?

The honest answers.

I'm not super "sporty." Will I survive?+
Yes. The physical activities are adapted to all fitness levels, and nothing is competitive. The goal is effort with support, not performance. You set the pace. The only things that are truly physically demanding are still walkable for anyone who can walk for a few hours.
I'm not into meditation. Should I still apply?+
If you're curious, yes. You don't need to love meditation to get something out of this — you just need to be open to trying. Many people who arrive skeptical say the quiet practices end up being the most impactful part. If you're 100% closed to it though, this probably isn't your project.
What if I really need my phone?+
You'll have it during travel, on arrival, and through the first morning of Day 1. Halfway through Day 1 we put phones in a box together — a small ritual we do as a group. You get yours back on the last workshop day. Throughout, your group leader keeps a phone for emergencies, and your family & friends at home get a number to reach you. You're reachable. You just aren't scrolling. If that's a dealbreaker, this isn't the project for you, and that's okay.
What if I get overwhelmed?+
Joyce is on site all week and you can always come to her for a one-on-one chat. There are dedicated quiet spaces. Every challenging activity is voluntary. You can always step out. You can always say "not this one." The whole team is trained to notice and check in.
Will I speak enough English?+
You don't need to be fluent. Most activities are body-based, or use simple instructions. Some of the most powerful sessions are non-verbal. Bring a willingness to try and you'll be fine.
Is it really free?+
Accommodation, meals, workshops, your travel (to a certain limit) and materials are covered by Erasmus+. You may want a bit of personal pocket money for the bakery stop, postcards, etc. That's it.
Can I extend my stay in France?+
Up to 7 days before or after the exchange, at your own expense. We can give you recommendations (Limoges, Paris, nature spots nearby).
